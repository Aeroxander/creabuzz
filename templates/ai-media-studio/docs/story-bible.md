# Story Bible

Fill this in with the team. It is the canon that @The Writer, @The Script Editor, @The Producer, @The Scene Generator, @The Sound & Music, and @The Continuity Keeper all work from: anything here is true for the piece, and anything not here is still open.

## Format

What are we making — feature film, series episode, music video, brand/ad film, audio drama? Note the runtime target and where it ships. Format sets the rules every other section answers to.

## Logline

One or two sentences: who wants what, what stands in the way, what's at stake.

> Example: A retired lighthouse keeper discovers the lamp only lights for people who are lost — and tonight, someone is.

## World & setting

- Where and when are we?
- What are the rules of this world — and which one does the story break?

## Characters

### [Name]

- **Want**: what they're chasing this piece
- **Need**: what they actually need (usually the opposite)
- **Voice notes**: how they talk — one habit is enough; for audio drama, add pace and register, since the ear is the only camera
- **Look**: keep this wording stable — @The Scene Generator reuses it verbatim so faces stay consistent between shots

## Tone & palette

Three adjectives for the piece, and two colors that own the screen (or the room, if it's audio-only).

## Sound & music

- **Score**: genre, instrumentation, and the one mood arc the music is allowed to travel.
- **World sound**: the textures that place us — room tone, weather, machinery, street.
- **Silence**: where the piece earns a hold, and what lands after it.

## First three scenes

What happens, in order. One line each. The first one is the one we shoot (or record) first.

## Ready to start? Three openers to try

Post one of these in #writers-room and mention @The Writer:

1. `@The Writer — here's my premise: [one sentence]. Give me three logline angles: one funny, one dark, one weird.`
2. `@The Writer — take the logline above and break it into a three-act beat sheet, with the midpoint turn named.`
3. `@The Writer — write the opening scene: [character] wants [X] in [setting], but [Y] happens instead. One page max.`

When a draft needs structure, format, or pacing work, hand it to @The Script Editor. When anything in this bible is contradicted by an asset, mention @The Continuity Keeper.
